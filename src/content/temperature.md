Let me preface by saying crafting new units of measurement is obviously a fool’s errand—it’s the adoption that gives it any meaning at all. Like language, and thousands of other things, shared understanding is the only reason it exists. Or is useful.

But as a fun thought experiment, I’m sure we could do better than Fahrenheit or Celsius.

## The problem

Many (sane) everyday SI units involves a multiple of 10 in a neat, prefixed fashion. Here’s a table I’m sure you’ve seen:

| Prefix | Factor |
| ------ | ------ |
| kilo   | 1,000  |
| hecto  | 100    |
| deca   | 10     |
| (base) | (base) |
| deci   | 0.1    |
| centi  | 0.01   |
| Milli  | 0.001  |

And so forth. Of course, the only other unit everyday people encounter that _doesn’t_ conform to this pattern is temperature. Annoyingly so. The upper bound on °C that the average person will encounter is approx. 40 °C, and the lower bound is approx. -10 °C. Not a big deal, of course. But we’re missing at least 60 degrees of utility here. As far as I'm aware, this is a side-effect of [the definition of the unit itself](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celsius). It’s a temperature system for water, not humans.

Fahrenheit is somewhat better. It’s origins are [somewhat contested](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fahrenheit), but it’s lower and upper bounds _were_ defined as the freezing point of a particular solution, and the best estimate of the human body temperature at the time. This was later changed to be fixed to (again) the freezing/boiling points of water with a 180 °F difference.

There’s a greater "resolution" to Fahrenheit here, since we’re essentially measuring the same states of water as Celsius, but with a few more fairy tale-esque stopping points along the way. t’s pretty common to see measurements above a 100 °F, and below 0 °F, which makes you wonder if we went too far. This is much closer to an ideal, normalized 0-100 scale, though.

All these measurements are great for scientific purposes (and Kelvin, obviously). But what about a temperature scale for humans?

## A modest proposal

Using a distribution of global temperatures.

Imagine if we did this for everything!
